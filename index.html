The 5 S's of Wine Tasting

Tasting like a professional isn't about snobbery; it's about engaging all your senses to isolate what elements bring you joy. Tap each step below to master the process:

Tilt your glass over a white background (like a napkin). Look for color depth. Light whites often lean crisp and young; deep golden whites may have oak age. Light, translucent reds (like Pinot Noir) suggest subtle body, while opaque purples hint at robust structure.
Keep the base of your glass flat on the table and spin it in small circles. This introduces oxygen into the wine, volatilizing the aroma compounds so they can float up into the bowl for you to smell.
Don't be shy! Stick your nose deep into the glass. Take a few short sniffs. Ask yourself: Is it fruity (lemon, peach, dark berries)? Floral? Earthy (mushroom, wet stones)? Or does it smell like baking spices or vanilla from wood aging?
Take a moderate sip and roll it around your entire mouth to coat all of your tastebuds. Think about the texture first, then the flavors. Draw in a tiny bit of air over the wine to help aerate it on your tongue.
After swallowing (or spitting), how long does the flavor linger? This is the "finish." Above all, make your personal choice: Was it refreshing, heavy, or balanced? Write down your rating and comments!

Your Palate Cheat Sheet

When reviewing your wines, you will rate core structural elements. Here is how to recognize them on your tongue:

🍋

Acidity (Mouth-Watering)

Gives wine its crispness, freshness, and "zing." Think of biting into a sour lemon slice; it makes your saliva glands flow.

🍂

Tannin (The Drying Sensation)

Found primarily in red wines. It leaves a dry, astringent feeling on your gums and tongue—similar to strong black tea.

🥛

Body (Weight & Viscosity)

How heavy or thick does the wine feel in your mouth? Think: Skim Milk (Light Body) vs. Whole Milk (Medium Body) vs. Heavy Cream (Full Body).
